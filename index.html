Ages of Siege

Hold the wall. Listen for the enemy. Headphones recommended.

Three eras so far: medieval, gunpowder and industrial.

Campaign

Each era is its own chapter. Clear every wave in an era to open the next one. Your gold, walls and defenses carry over, but a new era's weapons must be bought.

      Endless

      Survive as many waves as you can. You start with no upgrades and no gold, and shop between waves. One fallen wall ends the run. Every few waves a battering ram leads the attack.

      How to play

      You stand on the castle wall facing outward. Enemies march toward you in five lanes: far left, left, center, right, and far right. Each lane is heard in its own direction, so headphones work best. The closer an enemy is, the louder and clearer it sounds. Once it reaches the wall, it starts damaging it.

      Your crossbow hits the closest enemy in the lane you are aiming at. After each shot it needs a moment to reload. You will hear a ratchet click when it is ready. Firing early gives a dry click.

      Phone

      • Swipe left or right: aim one lane over. A tick plays in that lane, higher in pitch toward the right.
      • Tap: fire your main weapon.
      • Two-finger swipe up or down: switch between your main weapons (crossbow or musket, ballista or cannon, and the mortar).
      • Three-finger tap: fire the mortar.
      • Hold one finger still for half a second: pour boiling oil (medieval and gunpowder eras).
      • Hold one finger: fire the machine gun for as long as you hold (industrial era on).
      • Four-finger tap: fire the anti-aircraft gun.
      • Swipe up: status report.
      • Swipe down: repeat the last callout.
      • Two-finger tap: pause or resume.

      In the browser, turn VoiceOver off while you play a wave, because VoiceOver takes over the gestures. You can use your accessibility shortcut to switch it off and back on. The app version will not need this.

      Keyboard

      • Left and right arrows: aim one lane over.
      • 1 to 5: jump straight to a lane, from far left to far right.
      • F: fire the crossbow, or the musket once you own it. With the machine gun, hold F to keep firing.
      • A: fire the anti-aircraft gun.
      • B: fire the ballista, or the cannon, or the field artillery, whichever is your best.
      • M: fire the mortar.
      • Space: fire whichever main weapon is selected.
      • O: pour boiling oil.
      • S: status report.
      • R: repeat the last callout.
      • Escape or P: pause or resume.

      With JAWS, the battlefield is an application area, so your keys go straight to the game.

      Enemies

      • Foot soldiers: marching steps. One bolt each.
      • Knights: heavy steps and clanking armor. Three bolts each.
      • Ladder crews: quick running steps and rattling wood. When the ladder slams against the wall, shoot them before they climb over.
      • Battering ram: a deep rumble and a chanting crew, always aimed at the gate in the center. It booms against the gate for heavy damage.
      • Siege tower: a huge creaking tower on wheels. Archers on top fire volleys at the wall while it rolls in, and if it reaches the wall it drops a gangway and soldiers pour over.

      Gunpowder era enemies

      • Infantry: marching steps with rattling gear. Four damage each.
      • Musketeers: they stop about 60 meters out and fire volleys at the wall. Four damage each.
      • Cavalry: galloping hooves. Very fast, three damage each.
      • Sappers: quiet scraping underground. You can only hit them once they are within about 40 meters. If they reach the wall, they set off a mine.
      • Siege cannons: towed into place far out, then they fire heavy shots at the wall. Six damage to destroy one.
      • The grand battery: a great gun leading a line of siege cannons.

      Industrial era enemies

      • Riflemen: big groups squelching through the mud. Five damage each.
      • Armored cars: a fast engine whine. They stop about 40 meters out and spray the wall with machine-gun fire. Light weapons do half damage.
      • Gas troopers: they stop about 50 meters out and release a hissing gas cloud that eats at the wall until you kill them.
      • Tanks: a deep engine throb and clanking treads. Light weapons do one fifth damage. They fire their gun as they close in, and crush the wall if they reach it.
      • Zeppelins: a deep droning hum overhead. Only the anti-aircraft gun hits them properly; the machine gun and towers do a little. They drop bombs once close.
      • The landship: a giant tank with zeppelins above it.

      Weapons

      • Crossbow: quick to reload, hits the closest enemy in your lane.
      • Ballista: bought in the shop. Slow to reload, but its bolt pierces every enemy in the lane: two damage to the first enemy, one to each enemy behind it. You can fire it while the crossbow reloads, and the other way around. Three deep clacks mean it is ready.
      • Musket (gunpowder era): replaces the crossbow. Four damage per shot. Listen for the ramrod strokes, then the hammer click that means it is ready.
      • Cannon (gunpowder era): replaces the ballista. The ball bounces down the lane, four damage to the first enemy, then three, two and one. A bell rings when it is reloaded.
      • Mortar (gunpowder era): lobs a shell that lands where the closest enemy in your lane was standing, about two and a half seconds later, and hits everything near that spot for four damage. A falling whistle tells you where it will land.
      • Machine gun (industrial era): replaces the musket. Hold to fire a stream of bullets at the closest enemy in your lane. A rising hiss means it is getting hot; if it overheats it jams for a few seconds, then a double click means it is clear.
      • Field artillery (industrial era): replaces the cannon. Lands a second after firing on the closest enemy in your lane and hits everything within 20 meters.
      • Trench mortar (industrial era): a stronger, faster mortar.
      • Anti-aircraft gun (industrial era): fires at the closest aircraft in any lane.
      • Barbed wire (industrial era): slows riflemen and gas troopers about 20 meters out. Vehicles tear through it. It is repaired after each wave, like the wall.
      • Boiling oil: bought in the shop. Used in the medieval and gunpowder eras only. Pours on every enemy at the wall in your lane. It refills each wave. If nobody is at the wall in that lane, the pot is not used.

      Gold and repairs

      You earn gold for every kill and a bonus for clearing a wave. After each wave, repairs to the wall are paid automatically out of your gold, so the wall always starts the next wave at full strength. The less damage you take, the more you have to spend.

      If the wall falls in the campaign, you retry that wave with the upgrades you had. You can replay any wave you have already cleared to earn more gold, at half the usual reward.
